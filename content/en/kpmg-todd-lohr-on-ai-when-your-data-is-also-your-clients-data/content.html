KPMG is one of the world's largest professional services firms, with more than 276,000 employees providing audit, tax, and advisory services across 138 countries and territories. Todd Lohr, KPMG US Vice Chair and Head of Client Technology & Innovation, focuses on bringing AI to clients through products and platforms. He has worked on KPMG's AI capabilities for more than a decade.

Much of the data KPMG handles belongs to its clients, often in highly regulated industries. Lohr spoke with us about what that means for adopting frontier AI, how embedding enterprise safeguards can unlock AI capabilities and accelerate responsible deployment, and where Enterprise Frontier Safeguards will fit.

Why did KPMG decide to work with Anthropic?

We’ve followed Anthropic very closely since its founding, and its mission was intriguing from a partnership perspective because we share a lot of the same values.

At KPMG, trust is foundational. It's what clients expect from us across our core business of audit, tax, and advisory, and it's what underpins confidence in the capital markets.

What are frontier models changing about your business right now?

Ten years ago we were focused on using predictive analytics, machine learning, and bringing very bespoke applications of LLMs to our clients. The last three years have been marked by bringing general intelligence to both our clients and KPMG workforce. Now we're at another pivot point, where our strategy is to embed our industry and domain knowledge, like tax and legal, into AI platforms and products. We’re working with partners like Anthropic not only on how we help our clients disrupt their industries but also how we disrupt professional services more broadly.

Our tax business runs on Digital Gateway (opens in new tab), which we see as a market-leading platform for how we deliver services. With Claude now integrated into the platform, we’ve introduced Digital Gateway Powered by Claude (opens in new tab), bringing frontier AI directly into how we work and deliver for clients with greater intelligence, speed, and scale.

More capability means more value, but also more potential for misuse. How do you weigh the two?

I’m optimistic about what AI can bring to humanity and to businesses, from healthcare and longevity to agriculture. We’re already seeing AI create entirely new ways to serve our clients in all industries.

Healthcare is a good example. Emerging capabilities like ambient listening can generate clinician notes directly from patient conversations, and physicians tell us this type of tool could give them back hours each day. Clinicians remain firmly in the loop, and every hour they spend with patients rather than with systems is an hour that can improve care.

With that said, the same technology can create security and safety challenges. There are going to be people who use it for nefarious reasons. It’s critically important that as an industry we understand that, take an honest look at how it’s being used, and create a responsible, safe, and secure framework for how we deploy these technologies.

That’s why our Trusted AI framework (opens in new tab) is foundational to how we innovate at KPMG. As a professional services firm, we take seriously our responsibility to help organizations realize the value of AI safely and securely while preparing for what’s next. That includes rigorous testing, ongoing monitoring, human oversight, and independent assurance to help ensure AI systems remain transparent, reliable, and aligned to their intended purpose.

Additionally, we're using Claude Mythos to identify system vulnerabilities, and cyber AI more broadly to defend against them.. This is an area where we’re investing heavily alongside partners like Anthropic.

KPMG had an early look at Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (opens in new tab). What was your reaction?

It was positive to see how much thought and intention Anthropic put into the safeguards and working with partners on the program. Specifically, what stood out was data retention: how we will be able to keep the data within our organization and still have access to frontier models.

That's critically important for a firm like KPMG because we are the trustee of our clients' data in some of the most sensitive, regulated environments. We want our clients and firm to have the benefit of the powerful combination of that data and AI, but we need to do it at all times in view of how our clients want us to use their data, and what we can use it for.

What will Enterprise Frontier Safeguards let you do that you couldn't before?

Enterprise Frontier Safeguards will allow us to move faster, with confidence, in how we're able to use AI and where we can use it. One of the key tenets of the safeguards architecture will be the ability for us to retain data in cloud infrastructure we control.

Where we have regulated or highly sensitive data, having these safeguards built-in will allow us to confidently accelerate our AI deployment across those highly regulated and sensitive client engagements.

How will the automated misuse review in Enterprise Frontier Safeguards fit with KPMG's own Trusted AI program and risk processes?

As AI capabilities become more powerful and autonomous, organizations need the ability to identify potential misuse, emerging risks, and unintended outcomes at a scale that manual processes simply can't support. Automated misuse review helps provide that additional layer of oversight by continuously evaluating how AI systems are being used and flagging signs of serious misuse for our team to review.

We see automated misuse review as a natural extension of our Trusted AI approach and an enhancement to our existing risk management processes. Combining automated monitoring, KPMG human oversight, governance controls, and independent assurance creates a stronger and more resilient framework than any one approach alone.

What do you expect from AI vendors when it comes to safeguards?

Trust is paramount to our brand. It's literally our business model. We’re not going to make a trade-off between AI capability and trust. We expect both.

The more capable AI models become, the more important those safeguards become, and strong guardrails are what give organizations the confidence to put powerful AI to work at scale. We have an expectation with all of our vendors that safety, security, the safeguards around the use of AI, and alignment to our Trusted AI beliefs and programs are central to how we work together.

We have to strongly prioritize safeguards in every aspect of our business, and certainly in our use of AI.

What would you tell other leaders weighing frontier models right now?

You need to use these models. I can't think of a business or an industry that is not going to have some level of disruption from AI. "Wait and see" is not likely a sustainable answer for most organizations.

So, you have to adopt and secure at the same time. Think through the safety and security aspects, define the guardrails, and put them in place so you can move faster through the enterprise. Most organizations aren't going to have the luxury of not deploying these models. But they're going to have to do it in a way that's aligned to their business strategy and goals and done in a safe and secure manner.

What are you building with Claude next?

We've already started to put Claude to work to build AI-native ways of working in our business, specifically in tax and private equity. We've also built frontier AI cybersecurity services, and we’re using Claude Mythos to look at vulnerabilities across the enterprise, and then using AI to quickly patch and secure the enterprise against them.

What you're going to see in the next several months is an acceleration effect. As AI enables organizations to identify vulnerabilities and risks faster, AI will also become increasingly important to responding at that same speed.
